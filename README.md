## What Is EMDR Therapy?

**EMDR Therapy** is a structured psychological therapy used to help people process traumatic or distressing memories. Instead of simply talking about the past, EMDR uses a guided process where the client focuses on specific memories while the therapist uses bilateral stimulation, such as:

- Side-to-side eye movements
- Tapping
- Alternating sounds
- Other guided attention techniques

The purpose of EMDR is not to erase memories. Instead, it helps reduce the emotional intensity connected to those memories, making them feel less overwhelming in the present.

## How EMDR Therapy Works

EMDR therapy usually follows a structured process. A therapist will not normally begin with the most difficult memory immediately. First, they will spend time understanding your history, your current symptoms, and your emotional safety.

A typical EMDR therapy process may include:

### 1. Assessment

The therapist learns about your experiences, symptoms, and therapy goals. This helps them decide whether EMDR is suitable for you.

### 2. Preparation

Before trauma processing begins, the therapist may teach grounding techniques, relaxation methods, and emotional regulation skills.

### 3. Identifying Target Memories

You and your therapist identify the memories or experiences that may be connected to your current distress.

### 4. Reprocessing

During this stage, you focus on selected memories while following guided bilateral stimulation. The therapist supports you throughout the process.

### 5. Review

The therapist checks how the memory feels after reprocessing and whether the emotional intensity has reduced.

### 6. Ongoing Support

EMDR therapy continues at a pace that feels safe and manageable for you.

## Is EMDR Therapy the Same as Hypnosis?

No, EMDR therapy is not hypnosis.

During EMDR, you remain awake, aware, and in control. You can pause or stop at any time. A professional EMDR therapist should explain the process clearly before starting and make sure you feel safe throughout the session.
